# Code of conduct

LearnSDLC from SMU SCIS is software for people learning to build software. We want working on it to be as welcoming as we hope learning with it is. This code applies to everyone who takes part: in issues, pull requests, reviews, discussions and any other space of the project, and when someone represents the project elsewhere.

## What we expect

- Be kind and patient. Many people here are learning, and every one of us was a beginner once.
- Assume good intent, and ask before you conclude.
- Criticise work, not people. Say what is wrong with a change and how it could be better.
- Accept that a decision may not go your way, and that the maintainers have the last word on what is merged.
- Respect people's time: keep a thread to its subject, and say when you can no longer do something you offered to do.
- Protect other people's privacy, and learners' above all. Never post learner work, names, accounts or logs that identify a person.

## What is not acceptable

- Harassment of any kind, in public or in private.
- Insults, slurs, or demeaning remarks about anyone's identity, background, ability, age, body or level of experience.
- Sexual language or imagery, and unwelcome attention of any kind.
- Threats, or encouraging anyone to harm themselves or others.
- Publishing someone's private information without their clear permission.
- Deliberately disrupting discussions, or continuing after you have been asked to stop.

## How to report

If something here makes you or someone else unsafe or unwelcome, contact a maintainer privately through their GitHub profile, or open an issue that asks for a private way to reach the maintainers and holds no detail of what happened. Say what happened, where, and who was involved, with links if you have them. You may report on behalf of someone else.

Reports are read only by the maintainers, who keep the reporter's identity private. If a report concerns a maintainer, that maintainer takes no part in handling it.

## What the maintainers will do

The maintainers will acknowledge a report, look into it, and decide what to do. Depending on what happened, that may be:

1. a private word asking for the behaviour to change;
2. a written warning that names the behaviour and what follows if it happens again;
3. a temporary ban from the project's spaces;
4. a permanent ban.

The maintainers may remove, edit or reject comments, commits, code, issues and other contributions that break this code. They will tell the person concerned what was done and why, unless that would put someone at risk.
